About The Vault

The publication

What is The Vault Investigates?

The Vault Investigates is an independent evidence registry and accountability publication. It tracks how governments, NGOs, and political actors profit from the systems built to help the poor — extracting structured case files from primary sources across the United States, Puerto Rico, and the Philippines. Long-form analysis and investigation narratives publish on the Substack.

Who runs it?

The Vault is operated by a single investigative archivist under the pseudonym The Vault Archivist. The pseudonymous identity is a deliberate operational choice to protect source relationships and ongoing investigations. Background and credentials are available to verified press inquiries through the research access process.

What is TruthDrop.io?

TruthDrop.io is the operational gateway for The Vault Investigates. This site — hosted at vault.povertypimpslayerthevault.io — is the public-facing infrastructure: the registry, the tip submission channels, the research access application, and the editorial standards documentation. The Substack at thevaultinvestigates.cloud is where the long-form analysis publishes.

Is The Vault affiliated with any political party or organization?

No. The Vault investigates accountability failures regardless of which party or administration is responsible. It accepts no funding from government bodies, politically affiliated foundations, or organizations with a financial interest in the outcomes it covers. The work follows the evidence, not a political agenda.

The Registry

How the registry works

What is the evidence registry?

The registry is a structured index of case files, each built from primary source documentation — government records, court filings, DOJ press releases, audit reports, and formal inquiry records. Every entry is verified before publication. Nothing goes on record without a documentable source.

How is the registry populated?

Two sources: an automated DOJ press release scanner that extracts and structures case data on a regular run cycle, and manual case file entries added through editorial review. All entries — automated or manual — pass review before they publish publicly.

Why is the registry still seeding at launch?

The infrastructure is built and active. The DOJ scanner is completing its first run. Case files will appear as entries are extracted, reviewed, and cleared for publication. The Receipts page on the Substack carries the first published case file cards in the meantime.

Can I access the underlying documents?

Where documents are public record and legally reproducible, The Vault links and archives them directly. Restricted or sensitive documents may require a research access application. The Vault will not publish documents that put sources at risk.

Tips & Source Protection

Submitting information

How do I submit a tip?

Through the Drop a Tip page. Two secure channels are being finalized — ProtonMail for standard tips and Signal for sensitive or high-risk submissions. A fully anonymous Tor-based channel is in development for sources requiring maximum protection.

Do I have to identify myself?

No. Anonymous submissions are accepted. You do not need to provide your name, affiliation, or any identifying information. If you do not provide a contact method, The Vault cannot follow up — but your submission will still be reviewed and archived.

How does The Vault protect sources?

Source protection is active by default from the moment of first contact. The Vault will not voluntarily disclose a source's identity. Source contact information is maintained in systems separate from publication and registry infrastructure. See the full Source Protection Policy.

Will my tip definitely lead to a published investigation?

Not necessarily. Every submission is reviewed and archived, but not every submission leads to a published case file. Publication depends on whether the claims can be verified against primary sources. If The Vault cannot verify it, it will not publish it. Submissions that cannot be verified immediately are retained — they may become relevant as additional evidence arrives.

Contact & Access

Getting in touch

I'm a journalist. Can I collaborate with The Vault?

Yes. The Vault works with journalists whose reporting intersects with our registry scope. Apply through the Research Access page — include your publication, your current investigation, and what you are looking for.

I'm a researcher. Can I access the archive?

Researchers working on poverty-relief systems, NGO accountability, disaster recovery, or related subjects may apply for archive access. Applications are evaluated on research purpose and relevance to active files. Apply through the Research Access page.

How do I report an error in a published registry entry?

Factual corrections can be submitted through the tip channel on the Drop a Tip page. Corrections are reviewed against the primary record. The original claim and the correction both remain on record — corrections are never silent deletions.
