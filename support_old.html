Support

Sustaining independent investigative infrastructure.

The Vault operates without advertisers, without institutional funding, and without access agreements. The work continues because of people who believe it should.

What Support Funds

Specific costs, not abstract mission.

Independent investigative work has costs that institutional publications absorb invisibly. Without institutional backing, those costs are direct. Supporting The Vault funds:

  • Reporting time

    Investigative research is not fast. Following a paper trail, requesting records, cross-referencing sources, and building an evidence file takes time that cannot be compressed without compromising accuracy.

  • Secure infrastructure

    Encrypted communication tools, secure hosting, source protection systems, and the operational backend that handles tip intake and vetting.

  • Document archival

    Preserving primary source documents, evidence chains, and case file materials so the record survives regardless of what gets deleted elsewhere.

  • Legal review

    Published investigations that name actors and document accountability failures require legal review before publication. This is not optional for responsible journalism.

  • Source communication

    Maintaining secure, available channels for sources and whistleblowers — and responding to every submission that comes through them.

Why It Matters

No advertisers. No access agreements.

An investigation into how public money gets diverted cannot be funded by the institutions that divert it. That is not a principle — it is a structural requirement.

The Vault does not take advertising. It does not accept grants from government bodies or politically affiliated foundations. It does not have institutional backing that comes with access requirements or publication constraints.

That independence is not free. Supporters of The Vault are the operational reason it can publish without asking permission.
